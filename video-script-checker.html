Why the first two seconds decide everything

Instagram and YouTube both measure how fast people swipe away. If the first line does not give a reason to stay, the rest of the script never gets watched. Most creators spend hours on editing and thirty seconds on the hook — it is the other way around.

What this checker looks at

The hook. Length, whether it opens with a line everyone has heard before, whether it has a number, a curiosity word, a question and whether it speaks to the viewer as "aap".

The body. Whether it is long enough to teach something, whether it is broken into steps, and whether it talks to the viewer rather than about people in general.

The CTA. Whether you ask for one specific action instead of "like and subscribe", and whether there is a reason to do it now.

How to use the result

Fix the red lines first — they cost the most points. A score under 60 usually means the hook is doing the damage. Rewrite the first line, run it again, and compare. Two or three passes is normally enough to get past 80.

What a script needs before you upload

A script is not a speech you read out. It is a set of decisions made in advance so you do not have to make them while the camera is running. Before you press record, five things should already be on the page.

  • One promise. A single sentence that says what the viewer will be able to do, know or avoid by the end. If you cannot write it in one line, the video is two videos.
  • A hook that names the viewer. Not "aaj hum baat karenge" — that names the topic. Name the person: "Agar aapki reel 300 views par ruk jaati hai…" The viewer has to recognise themselves in the first line.
  • Proof. One thing that shows you are not guessing: your own numbers, a screen recording, a demo, a before-and-after, a receipt. Without proof the middle of the video is just opinion, and opinions do not hold attention.
  • An order. Steps, not a pile. Even a story needs a sequence: problem, attempt, failure, fix, result.
  • One call to action. A single next step, said once, near the end, with a reason attached. "Save this before your next upload" beats "like, share, subscribe and comment".

If any of the five is missing, the script is not ready — and the checker will usually catch it in the body or CTA score before you waste a shoot day.

The three-second hook test

Read your first line out loud, then run three checks on it. Any line that fails two of the three needs a rewrite.

  • The recognition test. Does it describe a situation the viewer is in right now? "Aapka resume 40 applications ke baad bhi reply nahi de raha" is recognisable. "Today we will talk about resumes" is not.
  • The open-question test. Does it leave a gap the viewer wants closed? A number with no explanation, a mistake with no name, a result with no method — all open gaps. A complete statement closes the gap and the viewer leaves.
  • The on-screen test. Can the same idea be shown as text in six words or fewer in the first frame? If the hook only works as a spoken paragraph, the muted viewer never receives it — and most viewers start muted.

A fast way to run the test on yourself: film the first five seconds, watch it with the sound off, then watch it once with the sound on. If the muted version is confusing and the spoken version is slow, the hook is doing neither job.

Retention structure: open a loop, pay it off

Attention is held by unfinished business. Open a question early, keep it open through the middle, and close it before you ask for anything. This is the skeleton that works for a 45-second reel and for a 12-minute tutorial — only the number of beats changes.

0:00-0:03  HOOK + LOOP 1
           "Teen galtiyan jo aapka resume reject karwa rahi hain.
            Teesri galti sabse common hai — woh last me."
0:03-0:12  PROOF
           Show the actual rejected application or the recruiter screen.
0:12-0:25  PAYOFF 1 + LOOP 2
           Mistake one and the fix. Then: "Par asli problem
            mistake two hai, aur woh dikhti nahi."
0:25-0:40  PAYOFF 2 and 3
           Fixes, in order, one line each. Keep the promised
           third mistake for the end, as you said you would.
0:40-0:45  CTA
           One action, one reason: "Save karo, agla resume
            bhejne se pehle ek baar dekh lo."

Two rules make loops work. First, never open more loops than you can close — three is plenty for a short video, and an unclosed loop is why viewers say a video "felt incomplete". Second, close the loop you opened in the hook before the CTA. If the payoff comes after the ask, the viewer leaves to find it somewhere else.

Common script problems and their fixes

  • Warm-up intro. "Hi guys, welcome back to the channel, aaj ka video bahut special hai." Fix: delete everything before the first useful sentence. Start on the promise.
  • Feature dump. Listing ten tips with no order or priority. Fix: pick three, rank them, and say which one matters most and why.
  • No proof. Claims without a screen, a number, a demo or a receipt. Fix: add one piece of evidence per claim, even if it is just your own dashboard.
  • Three CTAs. Like, subscribe, comment, follow, share, join the group. Fix: choose the one action that helps the viewer most and say it once.
  • Ending on "bas itna hi". Fix: end on the payoff restated in one line — the thing they should remember tomorrow.
  • Written to be read, not spoken. Long sentences with clauses stacked in the middle. Fix: read it aloud and cut every sentence you run out of breath on.

A worked before-and-after

Same topic, same length, same creator. The difference is structure, not effort.

BEFORE (no loop, no proof, three CTAs)
"Hi guys, welcome back. Aaj hum resume ke baare me baat
karenge. Resume bahut important hai. Aapko skills likhni
chahiye, experience likhna chahiye, aur formatting theek
rakhni chahiye. Agar video pasand aayi to like karo,
subscribe karo, comment karo. Milte hain next video me."
AFTER (loop opened, proof shown, one CTA)
HOOK      "Aapne 40 applications bheje aur ek bhi reply nahi
           aaya? Teen wajeh hoti hain. Teesri wajeh aapke
           control me nahi hai — par pehli do hain."
PROOF     Screen recording: the same CV sent twice, one
           rejected in a day, one shortlisted after a fix.
PAYOFF    One: photo and address on the CV. Two: a skills
           list with no numbers attached. Three: the role
           was already filled — not your fault, apply faster.
CTA       "Apna resume kholo, pehli do cheezein aaj hi
           hatao. Save karke rakh lo."

The after version is not longer. It just spends its first three seconds on the viewer's problem instead of on the channel, keeps a promise it made, and asks for one thing. That is the whole difference the score is measuring.

Frequently asked questions

Does it work for YouTube long videos?

Yes. The same rules apply: the first line has to earn the next ten seconds. It works for reels, shorts and long-form.

Does it write the script for me?

Is my script saved anywhere?

No. It never leaves your browser.

How long should a Reel script be?

Roughly 100–140 spoken words for 45 seconds. Read your draft aloud with a timer — that is more accurate than counting words, because pace varies with the edit.

Should I write the hook first or last?

Write the body first, then write the hook last. You cannot promise something you have not written yet, and a hook written last always matches what the video actually delivers.

Does using a script make videos sound robotic?

Only when it is read word for word. Write the hook and the payoff as full sentences, and keep the middle as short bullet points you speak from. That keeps the structure tight and the delivery natural.

Can I use this checker for a podcast or a webinar?

Yes. The three parts still apply: the opening line has to earn the next minute, the middle needs an order, and the ending needs one clear next step. Just allow a longer body.

What is a good score?

Above 80 is ready to shoot. Between 60 and 80, fix the hook first — it usually moves the score most. Under 60, rewrite the first line rather than patching the middle.
