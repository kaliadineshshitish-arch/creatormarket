How YouTube cuts your title on a phone

Most of your viewers will never see your full title. They see a slice of it, on a small screen, while walking to a bus stop. Where the title appears changes how much of it survives:

  • Phone home feed — one line, roughly the first 30–40 characters, then an ellipsis. This is where the majority of views start.
  • Search results — closer to the full 60–70 characters, because the row is wider and titles are allowed to wrap to two lines.
  • Suggested sidebar — small text, two lines at most, and it sits right next to a thumbnail that is already doing the visual work.
  • Desktop — the most generous space, and also the place where the fewest people will ever find you.

So design for the worst case, not the best one. Read your title, cover everything after the 35th character with your thumb, and ask whether the remaining words still make a promise. If they do not, the phone feed is showing a sentence that starts well and ends in "…" — and nobody taps an unfinished thought.

One more cut to remember: Shorts shelves and the "Up next" strip trim even harder. If a title only works when it is read in full, it does not work.

Keyword first: the rule and a worked example

The rule is simple. The words a viewer would type into YouTube must appear at the very start of your title, in the same order that person would say them. Not near the start. At the start.

Here is the workflow, using a real kind of video — a phone-only reels editing tutorial:

  • Step 1. Open YouTube and type "reel edit". Note what the search box suggests: "reel editing", "reel editing capcut", "reel editing tutorial in hindi". Those suggestions are typed by real people, so they are free keyword research.
  • Step 2. Pick the phrase that matches your video exactly. If you edit on CapCut on a phone, "reel editing capcut" is honest. "Reel editing course" is not, unless you actually teach a course.
  • Step 3. Put that phrase first, then add the specific promise, then the detail that proves it is real.

Same video, three drafts, with the keyword position measured from the first character:

Weak   64 chars, keyword at char 48
       "A video about how I finally learned to edit my reels much faster"

Better 52 chars, keyword at char 14
       "How I Edit a Reel in 10 Minutes (CapCut, Phone Only)"

Final  58 chars, keyword at char 1
       "Reels Editing: Cut a 30-Second Reel in 10 Minutes (CapCut)"

The weak version is not a bad video. It is a bad title: it spends 47 characters talking about itself before it says what the video is about. The final version leads with the search phrase, adds a time promise, and names the app so the right person knows it applies to them.

Run this test on your last five uploads. If the keyword sits past character 40 on any of them, that is a title you can rewrite today — changing a title on an old video costs nothing and search picks it up again.

Title formulas that work for Indian audiences

These are shapes, not templates to copy word for word. Each one works because it answers a question the viewer already has in their head. Keep the shape and change the specifics to match your video.

  • Price anchor. "₹500 Me Ghar Ka Studio Setup" or "₹15,000 Me Purana Phone, Naya Channel". Rupee figures stop the scroll because they are concrete, and they set an expectation you can actually meet. Only use a number that appears in the video.
  • Time-bound outcome. "30 Din Me Pehla Brand Deal" — the promise is a deadline, not a miracle. If it took you four months, say four months in the video and keep the title honest about the effort.
  • Local proof. "Sarojini Nagar Haul Under ₹1,000", "Pune Me ₹8,000 Ka 1BHK Kitchen Setup", "Delhi Metro Me Shoot Kaise Karein". A place name tells a viewer this video is about their life, not about a generic internet.
  • Confession with a lesson. "Main Ne 6 Mahine Barbaad Kiye — Yeh 3 Galtiyan". The loss is the hook; the three mistakes are the payoff. It works because the viewer wants to avoid your mistake, not admire your success.
  • Head-to-head. "Zomato Vs Swiggy: Delivery Partner Ki Real Income" or "Free Fire Vs BGMI On A ₹12,000 Phone". Two known things, one comparison, no explanation needed.
  • The question they already ask. "Kya ₹10,000 Ka Mic Kaafi Hai?" or "Kya 1000 Subscribers Par Monetisation Milta Hai?" Write the exact question that shows up in your comments and answer it in the video.
  • Beginner bridge. "Beginner Se Editor: 5 Cheezein Jo Main Pehle Seekhta". Names the starting point and the destination, so the viewer knows where they fit.

Two rules apply to all seven. First, the number or place must be real and visible in the video — a ₹500 claim over a ₹5,000 setup is the fastest way to lose a subscriber. Second, Hinglish works well for a mixed Indian audience, but keep the searchable phrase in the language people actually type. If viewers search "resume kaise banayein", a pure English title loses that search.

Title and thumbnail: who does what

A title and a thumbnail are one unit that gets read left to right, thumbnail first. They should never say the same thing twice — that wastes half your screen space.

  • The thumbnail carries the emotion. A face, a reaction, a product in hand, a before-and-after split. Three or four words of text at most, big enough to read at thumbnail size on a phone.
  • The title carries the specifics. The keyword, the number, the promise, the qualifier ("phone only", "under ₹1,000", "no editing"). These are the facts a thumbnail cannot hold.
  • The overlap is the waste. If the thumbnail already shouts "₹200 VS ₹2,000", the title should not repeat it. Spend those characters on the claim instead.

A working pair: thumbnail shows two headphones with "₹200 vs ₹2,000" printed on it; title reads "Sasta Headphone Bekar Hota Hai? Blind Test". The thumbnail sets up the question, the title says what the video does about it. Read together they make one sentence, and neither half is redundant.

Practical sizes: keep thumbnail text to 3–4 words, and keep the title to 6–9 words with the keyword first. If you cannot fit the idea in both halves, the video probably has two ideas in it.

Five title mistakes that cost clicks

  • Keyword at the end. "Yeh Sab Kuch Maine Sikha — Video Editing Tips" hides the searchable phrase past the phone cut. Move it to the front and the same video becomes findable.
  • Curiosity with no subject. "You Won't Believe What Happened" gives the viewer nothing to be curious about. Curiosity needs a named thing: what, where, how much, or who.
  • Two ideas in one title. "Resume Tips Aur Interview Preparation Aur Salary Negotiation" is three videos wearing one coat. Split it, or pick the strongest and save the rest.
  • Repeating the thumbnail. If the thumbnail says "₹500 SETUP", a title that also says "₹500 Setup" has spent its 60 characters saying nothing new.
  • Promising what the video does not deliver. A title that overclaims buys one click and loses the retention that click was supposed to earn. It also trains viewers to skip your next upload.

Fix them in this order: keyword position first, because it affects whether the video is found at all; honesty second, because it affects whether the channel grows; everything else third, because it is polish.

Does it write clickbait?

No. The titles are designed to be honest — they describe what the video actually delivers. Titles that lie get clicks once and then hurt your channel.

Can I use it for Shorts?

Yes, but Shorts titles are shorter. Pick the shortest title from the five and trim it to under 40 characters.

Will this rank my video?

No tool can promise ranking. It gives you a keyword-first title, a description that works as a snippet, and relevant tags — which is the part you control.

How long should a YouTube title be?

Aim for 55–60 characters. That keeps the whole title visible in search results and gets most of it into the phone feed. Anything past 60 characters is decoration for the few places that show it.

Should I put hashtags in the title?

No. Hashtags in a title eat the characters that should be carrying your keyword, and YouTube already reads them from the description. Keep the title for words a person would say out loud.

Can I change the title after publishing?

Yes, and you should when a video underperforms. Rewrite the first 40 characters around the phrase people actually search, then check the impressions in YouTube Studio over the next two to three weeks.

Does a Hindi title reach fewer people?

It reaches the people who search in Hindi, and that audience is large. The question is what your viewers type. Check the search suggestions for your topic in both languages and use whichever phrase appears there.

Should the title match the thumbnail text?

No. Split the job: let the thumbnail carry the emotion or the comparison, and let the title carry the keyword, the number and the specific promise. Repeating the same words wastes half the space.
