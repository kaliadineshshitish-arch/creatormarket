How to Sell Digital Products in India

Without a company, without GST, without a payment gateway rejecting you.

✓ UPI + PayPal✓ No company needed✓ Instant delivery✓ Real numbers

You can start selling digital products in India today with a PAN card, a UPI ID and a file people actually want. Everything else — the company, the GST number, the payment gateway — can wait until money is coming in.

This guide is the version I wish I had when I started. It is written from actually doing it, including the parts that did not work.

Step 1 — Make one product, not a store

Almost everyone builds the storefront first. It is the wrong order.

Pick one thing people already ask you about. A template, a checklist, a prompt pack, a small guide, a spreadsheet with formulas. The test is simple: would five strangers pay ₹99 for this? If the answer is "maybe", the product is not specific enough yet.

Specific beats broad every time. "A Notion template" sells badly. "A Notion freelancer tracker with invoice log and payment reminders" sells.

  • Good first products — prompt packs, checklists, templates, trackers, small guides, preset packs.
  • Bad first products — courses, memberships, anything that needs ongoing support.

Step 2 — Payments are the real hurdle in India

This is where most people get stuck, and it is not your fault.

Payment gateways in India are built for a business selling its own products. The moment you look like a marketplace — collecting money on behalf of other sellers — approvals get much harder. Some will simply decline and not tell you why.

Two routes that actually work

  • UPI direct. Put your UPI ID and a QR code on the checkout page. The buyer pays, the order is marked "awaiting confirmation", and you confirm it manually after checking your bank. Zero fees, zero setup, works today. The cost is manual confirmation.
  • PayPal for international. If anyone outside India wants to buy, PayPal is the least friction. You set a fixed INR-to-USD rate on the page and let them pay in dollars.

Running UPI for domestic buyers and PayPal for international ones on the same page is completely normal at this scale, and it is what a lot of small Indian stores do.

What about a gateway? Razorpay, Cashfree and PayU are all fine for a simple "I sell my own files" store. They get difficult when your model looks like a marketplace with commission. Start simple.

Step 3 — Delivery has to be automatic

Emailing files by hand does not scale past ten orders.

The pattern that works: store the file privately, keep the download link locked, and only reveal it once the order is marked paid. On Supabase, that means object storage plus row-level security so a buyer can only read their own order row.

  • Never put the file in a public folder with a guessable name.
  • Never send the raw file as an email attachment.
  • Give the buyer a download button that checks their order status first.

Step 4 — Paperwork, at the right time

Below the GST threshold you can operate as an individual with a PAN.

Keep every invoice from day one — buyers ask for them, and it makes you look like a business instead of a hobby. Once you cross the threshold, register. Do not spend money on incorporation before your first ten sales; spend it on the product.

Step 5 — The hard part is traffic, not tech

A store with no visitors sells nothing. This is the part nobody warns you about.

After the product exists, roughly 90% of your effort goes into getting people to the page. What actually moves the needle early:

  • Answering questions where your buyers already are — Reddit threads, Quora questions, creator groups. Helpful first, link second.
  • Free tools — a genuinely useful free tool brings people in and earns the right to show them a product.
  • Search — slow, but permanent. A page targeting a real query keeps working for years.

What does not work: posting the same link in ten places on day one. That gets you ignored, or banned.

What to expect, honestly

Your first month will probably make very little. That is normal.

Most people quit between the first product and the first sale, because the gap between them is silence. The ones who keep going are not smarter — they just did not stop at ten views.

A realistic sequence: one product, then 20-30 pieces of distribution (answers, posts, comments), then a second product. Somewhere in there the first sale happens.

Where to start today

  • Write down three things people have asked you for help with in the last month.
  • Pick the one you can turn into a single file in one evening.
  • Price it at ₹99 and put it behind a UPI QR code.
  • Tell ten people who have the problem, not ten friends.
